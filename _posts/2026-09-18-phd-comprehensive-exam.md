In February 2025, I passed the PhD Comprehensive Examination at the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, Concordia University. Here are a few things I wish I had known going in.

## What the Exam Actually Tests

The comprehensive exam is less about memorizing facts and more about demonstrating that you can think like a researcher. The committee wants to see that you understand the *why* behind the methods in your field, not just the *how*.

## How I Prepared

- **Built a reading list** of foundational papers in reinforcement learning and stochastic optimization — not just skimming abstracts, but understanding proofs and assumptions
- **Explained concepts out loud** — if I couldn't explain a topic clearly without notes, I didn't really understand it
- **Connected my own work to the broader literature** — knowing where your research sits in the field is essential

## The Hardest Part

The hardest part was not the technical questions — it was staying calm under pressure and being honest when I didn't know something. Saying "I'm not sure, but here's how I would approach finding out" is a perfectly valid answer.

## What Came After

Passing the exam was a significant milestone, but also a moment to reflect on direction. Research is a long game, and the exam is really just the beginning of independent scholarly work.
