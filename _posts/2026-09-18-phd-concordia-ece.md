I started my doctoral studies in September 2022 at the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering (ECE) at Concordia University in Montreal, Quebec. Here is an honest account of what that experience has been like — the structure, the environment, and what I wish I had known beforehand.

## About the Department

The ECE department at Concordia is one of the larger engineering departments at the university, with research groups spanning communications, signal processing, control systems, machine learning, and hardware design. The department sits within the Gina Cody School of Engineering and Computer Science, which has invested significantly in research infrastructure in recent years.

Montreal itself is a strong city for research — it hosts Mila (Quebec AI Institute), several world-class universities, and a growing tech ecosystem. This proximity shaped my own research path, including a period as a Research Assistant at Mila from September 2023 to April 2024.

## Program Structure

The ECE PhD program typically involves:

- **Coursework** — a set of required and elective graduate courses in your first year or two
- **Comprehensive Examination** — a major milestone that tests depth and breadth in your field; I passed mine in February 2025
- **Research** — the bulk of the program, working closely with your supervisor on original contributions
- **Thesis** — a written document presenting your research, followed by an oral defence

The program is research-intensive. Progress depends heavily on the relationship with your supervisor and how independently you can drive your own work forward.

## What I Researched

My doctoral work focused on **reinforcement learning and restless bandit methods for scheduling**, with applications to networked systems. Specifically, I worked on Age of Information (AoI) optimization in IoT networks — deciding which sensors to query at each time step to keep information as fresh as possible across the network.

This led to a publication at [IEEE SmartIOT 2025](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11311674).

## What I Learned

A few honest takeaways:

- **Independence is everything.** A PhD is not like coursework — there is no syllabus. You define the problem, you find the gaps, and you push the work forward yourself.
- **Read broadly, write early.** The instinct is to read until you feel ready to write. That day rarely comes on its own. Writing forces clarity.
- **The research community matters.** Attending seminars, talking to other students, and being part of the Mila community opened doors and shaped my thinking more than I expected.
